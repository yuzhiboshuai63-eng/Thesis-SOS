# AI-Generated Text Patterns in English Academic Writing

This document identifies typical patterns in AI-generated English academic text and provides strategies to humanize them.

---

## Sentence-Level Patterns

### 1.1 Sentence Length Distribution

| Characteristic | AI Text | Human Text |
|----------------|---------|------------|
| Average length | 20-25 words | 15-30 words (more varied) |
| Standard deviation | Low (3-5 words) | High (8-12 words) |
| Pattern | Uniform length | Varied with rhythm |

**Humanization Strategy**:
- Create deliberate variation: mix 8-word sentences with 35-word sentences
- Ensure standard deviation > 8 words
- Avoid clusters of similar-length sentences

### 1.2 Sentence Structure

| Pattern | AI Indicator | Humanization |
|---------|--------------|--------------|
| Subject-Verb-Object dominance | >75% of sentences | Increase passive voice, inversion, fragments |
| Compound sentences | Overly balanced structures | Use varied subordination |
| Rhetorical devices | Rare | Include occasional rhetorical questions, parallelism |

### 1.3 Common AI Sentence Starters

| High-Frequency Starter | AI Frequency | Human Frequency | Alternatives |
|------------------------|--------------|-----------------|--------------|
| "This study..." | Very high | Moderate | "We", "Our research", "The present work", "Here" |
| "It is important to note that..." | High | Low | "Notably", "Significantly", "Worth mentioning" |
| "Furthermore..." | High | Moderate | "Moreover", "In addition", "Also", "Beyond this" |
| "However..." | High | Moderate | "Yet", "Nevertheless", "That said", "Conversely" |
| "In conclusion..." | High | Moderate | "To conclude", "In summary", "Ultimately", "Finally" |
| "There is a need for..." | High | Low | "We need", "Research requires", "The field lacks" |

## Paragraph-Level Patterns

### 2.1 Paragraph Structure

| Feature | AI Text | Human Text |
|---------|---------|------------|
| Structure | Rigid "topic-evidence-conclusion" | Varied: narrative, analytical, argumentative |
| Opening | Always topic sentence | May start with example, question, or transition |
| Closing | Always summary sentence | May end with implication or open question |
| Length | Uniform (150-200 words) | Varied (80-300 words) |

### 2.2 AI Paragraph Patterns

```
AI Pattern:
[Topic sentence] + [Evidence 1] + [Evidence 2] + [Evidence 3] + [Concluding sentence]

Human Alternative:
[Hook/Example] + [Context] + [Argument] + [Evidence] + [Implication]
```

## Vocabulary Patterns

### 3.1 Overused Academic Words

| Word/Phrase | AI Frequency | Human Alternatives |
|-------------|--------------|-------------------|
| "significant" | Very high | substantial, considerable, notable, marked, meaningful |
| "important" | Very high | crucial, vital, essential, critical, key, central |
| "effective" | High | efficient, successful, productive, potent, efficacious |
| "various" | High | diverse, different, several, numerous, a range of |
| "many" | High | numerous, various, several, a number of, multiple |
| "some" | High | certain, particular, specific, select, individual |
| "however" | High | nevertheless, nonetheless, yet, though, although |
| "therefore" | High | thus, consequently, hence, as a result, accordingly |
| "in order to" | High | to, so as to, with the aim of, for the purpose of |
| "it is worth noting that" | High | notably, significantly, importantly, interestingly |

### 3.2 AI Collocation Patterns

| AI Pattern | Human Alternative |
|------------|-------------------|
| "play an important role" | "is crucial", "matters significantly", "is central" |
| "has a significant impact on" | "significantly affects", "substantially influences" |
| "there is a growing body of evidence" | "evidence increasingly suggests", "research increasingly shows" |
| "it is widely accepted that" | "scholars generally agree", "the field recognizes" |
| "this finding is consistent with" | "this aligns with", "this supports", "this confirms" |

## Semantic Patterns

### 4.1 Perplexity and Predictability

| Metric | AI Text | Human Text |
|--------|---------|------------|
| Perplexity | Lower (more predictable) | Higher (less predictable) |
| Next-word prediction | Easier | Harder |
| Expression | "Standard" choices | Occasional unexpected but valid choices |

**Humanization Strategy**:
- Use slightly unexpected but accurate word choices
- Avoid always selecting the most common synonym
- Occasional unconventional but correct phrasing

### 4.2 Burstiness (Sentence Complexity Variation)

| Feature | AI Text | Human Text |
|---------|---------|------------|
| Complexity | Uniform | Varied |
| Information density | Even distribution | Uneven (dense in key sections) |
| Depth | Consistent | Deeper in important sections |

## Humanization Techniques

### 5.1 Sentence-Level Techniques

```
Technique 1: Create Length Variation
  AI: "This method improves efficiency. It reduces costs. It enhances quality."
  Human: "This method improves efficiency dramatically. Costs drop. Quality? Enhanced significantly."

Technique 2: Use Contractions Sparingly
  AI: "It is important to note that..."
  Human: "It's worth noting..." (occasional use)

Technique 3: Add Personal Voice
  AI: "The results indicate that the method is effective."
  Human: "We found the method works well—better than expected, actually."

Technique 4: Use Rhetorical Questions
  AI: "This approach has several advantages."
  Human: "Why choose this approach? Several reasons stand out."
```

### 5.2 Paragraph-Level Techniques

```
Technique 1: Break the Formula
  AI: [Topic] → [Evidence] → [Conclusion]
  Human: [Question] → [Context] → [Argument] → [Evidence] → [Implication]

Technique 2: Vary Paragraph Length
  Mix short (50 words) and long (250 words) paragraphs

Technique 3: Unconventional Openings
  Start with: data, quote, question, anecdote, bold statement
```

### 5.3 Vocabulary Techniques

```
Technique 1: Replace AI Buzzwords
  "significant" → "substantial", "considerable", "notable", "marked"
  "important" → "crucial", "vital", "essential", "key"
  "effective" → "efficient", "successful", "productive"

Technique 2: Increase Variety
  Use different expressions for the same concept throughout

Technique 3: Use Field-Specific Idioms
  Include terminology and phrases common in your specific field
```

## Checklist for Humanization

- [ ] Sentence length varies significantly (SD > 8 words)
- [ ] Mix of short and long sentences
- [ ] Varied sentence structures (not all S-V-O)
- [ ] Limited use of "This study..." openings
- [ ] Replaced AI buzzwords with alternatives
- [ ] Varied transition words
- [ ] Personal voice occasionally present
- [ ] Paragraph structures varied
- [ ] Specific examples and details included
- [ ] Field-specific terminology used
